Tea moves from Drinks to Gifts on 10 January. A sale made on 9 January reaches the warehouse on 12 January. A report of categories at the time of sale must assign that line to Drinks, even though the current catalogue says Gifts.

## Keep the version the event needs

A **slowly changing dimension** describes a business object whose attributes can change. A type 1 update replaces the attributes on an existing row. It makes old facts display the current category. This can be useful for correcting a misspelling when no report needs the old spelling. A type 2 change inserts a new row with a new **surrogate key**. The durable product key ties its versions together, while the surrogate key identifies the version linked to a fact. ([Kimball, 2008](#references); [Oracle, §2.4.2](#references))

Use version 7 for Tea in Drinks and version 8 for Tea in Gifts. The first is valid from 1 January up to 10 January, and the second starts on 10 January. Treat intervals as inclusive at the start and exclusive at the end. A sale exactly on 10 January then matches the new version once. With two inclusive intervals it could match both.

## Look up by the sale date

The warehouse receives sale 101 on 12 January. Its event date is 9 January. The type 2 lookup uses the event date and the durable product key. After finding the version, the fact stores that version's key. Here dates are sufficient because the change occurs at a day boundary in the reporting calendar. An intraday rule needs instants and a time-zone convention. ([Microsoft, Slowly changing dimensions](#references))

```sql
WITH product_versions(version_id, product, category, valid_from, valid_to) AS (
    VALUES
        (7, 'tea', 'Drinks', DATE '2026-01-01', DATE '2026-01-10'),
        (8, 'tea', 'Gifts',  DATE '2026-01-10', NULL::date)
), sales(order_id, product, sold_on, arrived_on, cents) AS (
    VALUES
        (101, 'tea', DATE '2026-01-09', DATE '2026-01-12', 700),
        (102, 'tea', DATE '2026-01-10', DATE '2026-01-10', 350)
)
SELECT s.order_id, d.version_id, d.category, s.cents
FROM sales AS s
JOIN product_versions AS d
  ON d.product = s.product
 AND d.valid_from <= s.sold_on
 AND (s.sold_on < d.valid_to OR d.valid_to IS NULL)
ORDER BY s.order_id;
```

The result is 101 with version 7 and 700 cents under Drinks, then 102 with version 8 and 350 under Gifts. Using `arrived_on` instead would put both under Gifts. Overlapping intervals can duplicate a sale. A gap can make an inner join drop it. Before loading the foreign keys, check that each sale has exactly one match, for example with a left join and a count of matching version IDs. A missing version may require deferring the fact or representing it explicitly as unresolved.

## Preserve effective time and recorded time

A category change has a **valid time** when it takes effect in the shop. The warehouse also has a time when it learned that information. A correction received on 13 January might say that the change was effective on 8 January. A report that shows corrected history can revise the intervals and reassign affected facts. A report that must reproduce yesterday's published answer also needs the earlier recorded version or a retained published snapshot. One effective-time interval per version alone cannot answer both questions.
