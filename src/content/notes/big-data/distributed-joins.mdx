The report adds a product category to each sales line. The [local join algorithms](/notes/databases/join-algorithms) can match a line to a catalogue row once both are on one worker. Sales are placed by store, so the appropriate catalogue entry may be on another worker. The plan needs a rule for where the two inputs meet.

## Keep rows together or copy the small side

A **colocated join** runs locally if both inputs are already partitioned by compatible rules on the join key. If the fact and catalogue tables use the same hash of `product_id` and the same destination mapping, matching keys meet without moving either input for the join. A later stage can still require an exchange. Partitioning sales by product to help this join can make a report by store need more movement. ([Greenplum 6, Distribution and skew](#references))

The catalogue may be small enough to **broadcast**. Suppose its projected payload is 60 MB, initially divided among four workers with one copy of each row. Each worker keeps its part and sends it to the other three. The transferred payload is $3 \cdot 60=180$ MB in this model. Every worker can build a local lookup using the full catalogue and join its own sales. The catalogue copy and lookup structure must fit each worker's budget. A 60 MB transfer representation does not determine the size of the in-memory structure. ([Greenplum 6, Distribution and skew](#references); [Spark 3.5.8, Join strategy hints](#references))

## Repartition both inputs by the join key

If the catalogue cannot be replicated within that budget, repartition both inputs by `product_id`. Each key is sent to one destination, so the worker there can run a local join. Use 600 GB of sales and the 60 MB catalogue to compare payloads. Assume four destinations, a uniform hash and initial placement independent of the target. One quarter of the bytes stay local in expectation. About $0.75\cdot600=450$ GB of sales and $0.75\cdot60=45$ MB of catalogue data cross workers. If one table is already correctly partitioned, its movement can be avoided. These estimates omit compression and temporary I/O. ([Schneider and DeWitt, 1989, §3](#references))

## A key can dominate its partition

Hashing spreads different keys. It cannot divide a single key among multiple destinations under the stated rule. If half the sales payload belongs to one product, the destination for that product receives at least 300 GB of sales data, counting its existing local rows. A skew-aware plan can sometimes split the large matching group and duplicate the needed rows of the other side. It must still return each matching pair once. Broadcast avoids moving the sales by key, so it can preserve a useful balance already present in their store partitions. ([Greenplum 6, Distribution and skew](#references); [Spark 3.5.8, Adaptive query execution](#references))
