A generative adversarial network learns a generator through a classification game. The discriminator learns to distinguish real data from generated samples; the generator learns to make that distinction harder.

## Generator and discriminator

The generator maps noise into data space:

$$
z\sim p_z,\qquad \tilde{x}=G_{\theta_g}(z).
$$

The distribution of generated samples is $p_g$. It is induced by sampling $z$ and applying $G$; the noise distribution $p_z$ and the output distribution $p_g$ are different objects.

The discriminator outputs $D_{\theta_d}(x)\in(0,1)$, interpreted as the probability of a real-data label. Real examples receive $y=1$ and generated examples receive $y=0$ when training $D$.

## The minimax value function

The discriminator maximizes the log-probability of correct real/fake decisions. The generator minimizes the same value:

$$
\min_G\max_D V(G,D),
$$

$$
V(G,D)
=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}}[\ln D(x)]
+\mathbb{E}_{z\sim p_z}[\ln(1-D(G(z)))].
$$

This is the minimax game described in the notes: one player increases its payoff, while the other tries to decrease it. The real-data term does not depend on $G$ when $D$ is held fixed.

For discrete variables, these expectations become probability-weighted sums:

$$
V(G,D)
=\sum_x p_{\mathrm{data}}(x)\ln D(x)
+\sum_z p_z(z)\ln(1-D(G(z))).
$$

For continuous variables with densities, the corresponding form is

$$
V(G,D)
=\int p_{\mathrm{data}}(x)\ln D(x)\,dx
+\int p_z(z)\ln(1-D(G(z)))\,dz.
$$

The notes alternate between sums, integrals and minibatch averages to express the same underlying expectations. Here all logarithms are natural logarithms.

## Binary cross-entropy and loss signs

For a binary label $y$ and predicted probability $q$, binary cross-entropy is

$$
\operatorname{BCE}(y,q)
=-\bigl[y\ln q+(1-y)\ln(1-q)\bigr].
$$

Thus a real sample contributes $-\ln D(x)$ and a generated sample contributes $-\ln(1-D(G(z)))$ to the discriminator's loss:

$$
L_D
=-\mathbb{E}_{x\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}}[\ln D(x)]
-\mathbb{E}_{z\sim p_z}[\ln(1-D(G(z)))].
$$

Minimizing $L_D$ is equivalent to maximizing $V$. Averaging the two class contributions instead of adding them changes only a positive scale factor.

<aside class="callout"><strong>Editorial correction.</strong> Page 22 switches between BCE and the log-likelihood payoff without consistently preserving the minus signs. The positive log-probability expressions belong to the payoff maximized by $D$; BCE is their negative and is minimized.</aside>

## Alternating updates

The training sketch fixes one network while updating the other:

1. **Update $D$:** sample real examples and noise, generate fake examples, then ascend the minibatch estimate of $V$ or descend $L_D$.
2. **Update $G$:** hold the discriminator's parameters fixed, sample noise, and descend the generator loss.
3. Repeat. Several discriminator updates per generator update are possible.

The original minimax generator loss is

$$
L_G^{\mathrm{MM}}
=\mathbb{E}_{z\sim p_z}[\ln(1-D(G(z)))].
$$

<aside class="callout"><strong>Editorial clarification.</strong> Holding $D$ fixed during the generator update freezes its parameters, but the gradient still passes through $D(G(z))$ to the generator. During the discriminator update, generated samples are treated as inputs rather than used to update $G$.</aside>

## The optimal discriminator

For a fixed generator, we can replace the expectation over noise with an expectation over its generated samples:

$$
V(G,D)=\int
\left[
p_{\mathrm{data}}(x)\ln D(x)
+p_g(x)\ln(1-D(x))
\right]dx.
$$

At each $x$, maximize $a\ln d+b\ln(1-d)$ with $a=p_{\mathrm{data}}(x)$ and $b=p_g(x)$. Its derivative is

$$
\frac{a}{d}-\frac{b}{1-d}=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
d=\frac{a}{a+b}.
$$

The second derivative, $-a/d^2-b/(1-d)^2$, is negative wherever $a,b>0$, so this stationary point is a maximum. Consequently,

$$
D_G^*(x)
=\frac{p_{\mathrm{data}}(x)}
{p_{\mathrm{data}}(x)+p_g(x)}.
$$

Where only one distribution has positive density, the optimum is the corresponding boundary value, $1$ or $0$. Where both densities are zero, the objective places no constraint on $D$.

<aside class="callout"><strong>Editorial correction.</strong> The opening remark on page 21 says “if generator is optimal.” The JS reduction below requires the <em>discriminator</em> to be optimal for the current generator. This is also the fixed-$G$ calculation shown on page 23.</aside>

## Why Jensen–Shannon divergence appears

Let $C(G)=V(G,D_G^*)$ denote the value after maximizing over $D$. Substitution gives

$$
C(G)
=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}}
\left[\ln\frac{p_{\mathrm{data}}(x)}
{p_{\mathrm{data}}(x)+p_g(x)}\right]
+\mathbb{E}_{x\sim p_g}
\left[\ln\frac{p_g(x)}
{p_{\mathrm{data}}(x)+p_g(x)}\right].
$$

Define the equal-weight mixture $m=(p_{\mathrm{data}}+p_g)/2$. Each denominator above is $2m(x)$, so

$$
\begin{aligned}
C(G)
&=\mathbb{E}_{p_{\mathrm{data}}}
\left[\ln\frac{p_{\mathrm{data}}(x)}{m(x)}\right]
+\mathbb{E}_{p_g}
\left[\ln\frac{p_g(x)}{m(x)}\right]
-2\ln2\\
&=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{\mathrm{data}}\|m)
+D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_g\|m)-2\ln2\\
&=2D_{\mathrm{JS}}(p_{\mathrm{data}}\|p_g)-\ln4.
\end{aligned}
$$

The JS divergence is nonnegative and vanishes exactly when the distributions agree. The ideal global minimum is therefore

$$
p_g=p_{\mathrm{data}},\qquad
D_G^*(x)=\frac12,\qquad
C(G)=-\ln4.
$$

The equality above describes the original minimax game with an optimal discriminator and sufficient model capacity. It does not guarantee that finite neural networks and alternating gradient updates will reach the ideal solution. The [original GAN paper, sections 3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/1406.2661v1#S3), distinguishes practical alternating updates from its theoretical analysis.

## Non-saturating generator loss

Pages 24–25 ask which loss the code uses. If the discriminator confidently rejects generated samples, $D(G(z))$ is close to zero. The original minimax loss then gives a weak gradient through the sigmoid output.

The alternative is to maximize $\ln D(G(z))$, or equivalently minimize

$$
L_G^{\mathrm{NS}}
=-\mathbb{E}_{z\sim p_z}[\ln D(G(z))].
$$

This trains $G$ using a real-data target for generated samples. Those samples still receive the fake target when training $D$.

To see the gradient difference, write $q=\sigma(a)$, where $a$ is the discriminator's logit for one generated sample:

$$
\ell_{\mathrm{MM}}(a)=\ln(1-\sigma(a)),
\qquad
\frac{d\ell_{\mathrm{MM}}}{da}=-q,
$$

$$
\ell_{\mathrm{NS}}(a)=-\ln\sigma(a),
\qquad
\frac{d\ell_{\mathrm{NS}}}{da}=q-1.
$$

When $q\approx0$, the minimax derivative approaches zero while the non-saturating derivative approaches $-1$. These are derivatives with respect to the logit: the complete parameter gradient also includes the derivative of the discriminator logit $a(G(z))$ with respect to the generator's parameters.

## Conditional GAN: notebook context

The scan names conditional GANs but gives no detailed conditional objective. The related notebook's cells 17–26 supply this practical extension: both networks receive a class label $c$,

$$
\tilde{x}=G(z,c),\qquad D(x,c).
$$

The corresponding adversarial value is

$$
V_{\mathrm{cond}}
=\mathbb{E}_{(x,c)\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}}[\ln D(x,c)]
+\mathbb{E}_{c\sim p_c,\;z\sim p_z}
[\ln(1-D(G(z,c),c))].
$$

This equation formalizes the notebook's conditioning description. Its CIFAR100 implementation embeds the label and concatenates it as an additional spatial channel: at the generator's latent grid and at the discriminator's image input. The label provides control over which class to generate.

## f-GAN: notebook context

The scan names f-GAN and later mentions the Fenchel conjugate. The notebook's cells 27–33 extend the game to other divergences. For a convex function $f$ with $f(1)=0$,

$$
D_f(P\|Q)=\int q(x)f\!\left(\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}\right)dx,
\qquad
f^*(t)=\sup_u\{ut-f(u)\}.
$$

The variational formulation trains a function $T$ through

$$
\min_G\max_T
\left\{
\mathbb{E}_{x\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}}[T(x)]
-\mathbb{E}_{z\sim p_z}[f^*(T(G(z)))]
\right\}.
$$

Restricting $T$ to a neural network gives a lower bound on the divergence. Its output must remain in the domain of $f^*$, which motivates the notebook's divergence-specific output transforms. These equations are an editorial expansion from [the f-GAN paper, sections 2.1–2.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.00709), rather than a full derivation present in the scan.

Cell 31 selects the JSD option. Cell 32 uses a generator surrogate that maximizes $T$ on generated samples; the direct minimax fake term is retained as a commented alternative. Thus the divergence selected for the variational objective and the generator update actually used in code should be distinguished.

## Evaluation question

Page 24 leaves a question about computing FID by class or repeatedly from different samples. FID is an evaluation statistic, while BCE supplies the training losses above. A per-class FID conditions the compared datasets on class; repeated sampling estimates how a finite-sample score varies. The [FID note](/notes/generative-models/fid) explains its feature-distribution comparison.
