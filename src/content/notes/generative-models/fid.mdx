**Fréchet Inception Distance (FID)** compares a generated image set with a reference image set. The seminar's central distinction from Inception Score is that FID actually uses the real data: it compares the two sets in a learned feature space.

## From images to feature distributions

Pass every image through the same fixed, pretrained feature extractor. Standard FID uses the 2,048-dimensional output of the last pooling layer of Inception-v3, before its classification head. These are **features**, not softmax class probabilities.

Write the feature vector of an image as $f(x)$. For real and generated images, estimate the means and covariance matrices:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_r &= \frac{1}{N_r}\sum_{i=1}^{N_r} f(x_i), \\
\Sigma_r &= \frac{1}{N_r-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N_r}
(f(x_i)-\mu_r)(f(x_i)-\mu_r)^\top, \\
\mu_g &= \frac{1}{N_g}\sum_{j=1}^{N_g} f(\tilde{x}_j), \\
\Sigma_g &= \frac{1}{N_g-1}\sum_{j=1}^{N_g}
(f(\tilde{x}_j)-\mu_g)(f(\tilde{x}_j)-\mu_g)^\top.
\end{aligned}
$$

The covariance estimators above require at least two samples per set. The subscript $r$ denotes the real reference and $g$ denotes generated images; an image in one set does not need a paired image in the other.

FID summarizes each feature distribution with a multivariate Gaussian:

$$
\mathcal{N}(\mu_r,\Sigma_r)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\mathcal{N}(\mu_g,\Sigma_g).
$$

This is an approximation to the distribution of features. It does not assert that natural images, or even their Inception features, really follow a Gaussian.

## The distance between the two Gaussians

FID is the **squared 2-Wasserstein distance** between these Gaussian summaries:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{FID}
&= \|\mu_r-\mu_g\|_2^2 \\
&\quad + \operatorname{tr}\!\left[
\Sigma_r+\Sigma_g
-2\left(\Sigma_r^{1/2}\Sigma_g\Sigma_r^{1/2}\right)^{1/2}
\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

The first term measures a shift of the feature center. The trace term measures a mismatch in the spread and correlation structure. The symmetric matrix inside the inner square root makes the expression valid even when the covariance matrices do not commute.

Here $A^{1/2}$ means a **matrix square root**, not the square root of each entry. For a symmetric positive semidefinite matrix, it is the symmetric positive semidefinite square root. The trace is the sum of the diagonal entries.

The source notes and the original FID paper use the shorter trace expression with $(\Sigma_r\Sigma_g)^{1/2}$. For positive definite covariances, its trace agrees with the symmetric form above. This does not let us replace it by $\Sigma_r^{1/2}\Sigma_g^{1/2}$ in general; matrix multiplication is not commutative.

For a one-dimensional Gaussian, the formula becomes particularly simple:

$$
\operatorname{FID}_{\text{1D}}
= (\mu_r-\mu_g)^2 + (\sigma_r-\sigma_g)^2,
$$

where $\sigma_r$ and $\sigma_g$ are **standard deviations**. Matching the mean is insufficient if the spread differs, and matching the spread is insufficient if the mean differs. This one-dimensional example explains the two terms; standard image FID uses the full feature vectors.

## How to read the value

**Lower is better under a fixed evaluation setup.** A smaller value means the fitted feature Gaussians are closer. FID has no universal threshold that makes an image generator good.

In exact arithmetic, FID is nonnegative. Zero means that the two Gaussian summaries have the same mean and covariance. The handwritten phrase “perfect match” should be read at this level: different non-Gaussian feature distributions can share these two moments, and matching feature statistics does not prove that the underlying image distributions are identical.

FID evaluates sets of images. It does not identify which particular generated image is good or bad, and it does not separate image fidelity and diversity into independent scores. Both can affect the feature statistics.

## What must stay consistent

The scan lists three practical qualifications:

- **Gaussian approximation.** Two moments discard information about the rest of the feature distribution, including some differences between its modes.
- **Feature relevance.** Inception was trained for ImageNet classification. Its representation can miss distinctions that matter in a different image domain.
- **Sampling and preprocessing.** Means and covariances are estimated from finite sets. Changing the number of images, the reference set, resizing or normalization can change the reported value.

The notes recommend thousands of images. This is a practical warning about noisy estimates, not a sufficient sample count for every dataset. Use the same sampling protocol and report the real and generated sample counts when comparing models. A single small-batch FID should not be treated as the population distance.

For reproducible comparisons, keep the feature model and layer, reference split, sample counts and image preprocessing fixed. Use a documented implementation for the covariance square root; singular covariance estimates and floating-point error need numerical handling.
