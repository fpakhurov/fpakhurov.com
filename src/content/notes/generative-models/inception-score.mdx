An image generator should produce recognizable images and a varied collection of them. **Inception Score (IS)** turns these two aims into a statistic of a fixed image classifier's predictions. The seminar describes the intuition as: each image should distinctly resemble something, while the set should contain variety, such as different dog breeds.

The metric was introduced by [Salimans et al., *Improved Techniques for Training GANs*, section 4](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2016/file/8a3363abe792db2d8761d6403605aeb7-Paper.pdf). A pretrained Inception classifier supplies the label probabilities. Higher IS is preferred under the same evaluation setup.

## Conditional predictions and their marginal

Generate many images $x_1,\ldots,x_N$ and pass each through the fixed classifier, typically an Inception model pretrained on ImageNet. Its softmax gives a distribution over $K$ labels:

$$
p(y\mid x),\qquad y\in\{1,\ldots,K\}.
$$

This is the **conditional label distribution for one image**. It expresses the classifier's beliefs, rather than a verified ground-truth label.

The **marginal label distribution** averages those predictions over the generated images. If $X\sim p_g$ is a generated image, then

$$
p(y)=\mathbb{E}_{X\sim p_g}[p(y\mid X)].
$$

This is the law of total probability. In the discrete case,

$$
\Pr(Y=y)=\sum_x \Pr(Y=y\mid X=x)\Pr(X=x).
$$

For a continuous image distribution, the sum is replaced by an integral. We can understand $Y$ as a label drawn from the classifier's probability vector after drawing $X$. The generator determines how often images occur; the classifier determines the conditional label probabilities.

For an empirical set of generated images, each sample receives weight $1/N$:

$$
\widehat{p}(y)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}p(y\mid x_i).
$$

The average uses the full probability vectors, rather than only their highest-probability labels.

## Confidence within an image, diversity across images

Using natural logarithms, an image's conditional entropy is

$$
H(p(\cdot\mid x))=-\sum_y p(y\mid x)\ln p(y\mid x).
$$

Low conditional entropy means that the classifier concentrates its probability on one label. The intended interpretation is a recognizable object. This remains a proxy: a classifier can be confidently wrong.

Across generated images, the marginal entropy is

$$
H(p(\cdot))=-\sum_y p(y)\ln p(y).
$$

High marginal entropy means the predictions spread across labels. Its maximum is $\ln K$ when the marginal is uniform. A generator producing one confidently recognized category has low conditional entropy, but also low marginal entropy.

The quantity we want to increase combines both requirements:

$$
H(p(\cdot))-\mathbb{E}_{X\sim p_g}[H(p(\cdot\mid X))].
$$

The first term rewards label variety; the second subtracts uncertainty within each image.

## Deriving the KL form

Start by expanding the two entropies:

$$
\begin{aligned}
H(p(\cdot))-\mathbb{E}_X[H(p(\cdot\mid X))]
&=-\sum_y p(y)\ln p(y)\\
&\quad+\mathbb{E}_X\!\left[\sum_y p(y\mid X)\ln p(y\mid X)\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

Substitute $p(y)=\mathbb{E}_X[p(y\mid X)]$ into the coefficient of the first term. The marginal $p(y)$ inside the logarithm is fixed with respect to $X$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-\sum_y p(y)\ln p(y)
&=-\sum_y \mathbb{E}_X[p(y\mid X)]\ln p(y)\\
&=-\mathbb{E}_X\!\left[\sum_y p(y\mid X)\ln p(y)\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

This step uses linearity of expectation and the finite sum over labels:

$$
\sum_y \mathbb{E}_X[f_y(X)]
=\mathbb{E}_X\!\left[\sum_y f_y(X)\right].
$$

Factor the shared expectation and combine the logarithms:

$$
\begin{aligned}
H(p(\cdot))-\mathbb{E}_X[H(p(\cdot\mid X))]
&=\mathbb{E}_X\!\left[\sum_y p(y\mid X)
\bigl(\ln p(y\mid X)-\ln p(y)\bigr)\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}_X\!\left[\sum_y p(y\mid X)
\ln\frac{p(y\mid X)}{p(y)}\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}_X\!\left[D_{\mathrm{KL}}
\bigl(p(\cdot\mid X)\,\|\,p(\cdot)\bigr)\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

The same quantity is the mutual information $I(X;Y)$ for the joint distribution defined by the generator and the classifier. IS exponentiates it:

$$
\operatorname{IS}
=\exp\!\left(\mathbb{E}_{X\sim p_g}
\left[D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(p(\cdot\mid X)\,\|\,p(\cdot)\bigr)\right]\right)
=\exp(I(X;Y)).
$$

Here, $0\ln 0=0$. A label with marginal probability zero also has conditional probability zero for almost every generated image, so it contributes zero.

In the second row, a uniform marginal is insufficient: every image is equally ambiguous. Only the third row combines sharp predictions with variety across images. The exact zero and one probabilities describe limiting cases of a softmax classifier.

## Logarithm bases and bounds

The logarithm base sets the information unit. If KL uses natural logarithms, IS uses $\exp$. If KL uses base-2 logarithms, the matching exponentiation is base 2:

$$
\exp\!\left(\mathbb{E}_X[D_{\mathrm{KL},e}]\right)
=2^{\mathbb{E}_X[D_{\mathrm{KL},2}]}.
$$

Combining base-2 KL with $\exp$ would give a different numerical score. The number of labels does not determine the logarithm base.

For $K$ labels,

$$
0\le I(X;Y)\le H(Y)\le\ln K,
\qquad
1\le\operatorname{IS}\le K.
$$

The lower bound occurs when the conditional label distribution does not depend on the image. The upper bound requires deterministic predictions and a uniform marginal over all $K$ labels. The bound $\ln K$ applies to the information before exponentiation, while $K$ bounds the score itself.

## Estimation and interpretation

For a finite generated set, the empirical score is

$$
\widehat{\operatorname{IS}}
=\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_y
p(y\mid x_i)\ln\frac{p(y\mid x_i)}{\widehat p(y)}\right).
$$

The original paper emphasizes using many generated samples, evaluating with 50,000. Its [released implementation](https://github.com/openai/improved-gan/blob/master/inception_score/model.py) computes scores on ten splits and reports their mean and standard deviation. Each split estimates its own marginal, so that reported mean is a different statistic from one score computed over the entire set.

Record the classifier checkpoint, image preprocessing, sample count and split protocol when comparing results. Changing the classifier or the label vocabulary changes what confidence and diversity mean.

The definition also explains several limits:

- **No real-data comparison.** IS uses generated images and a fixed classifier; it does not compare their distribution with the target dataset.
- **Limited diversity signal.** Variation that the classifier maps to the same probability vector is invisible to the score. Repeating one representative image per label can still give a high IS.
- **Classifier-dependent quality.** Confident predictions do not guarantee realistic images, especially outside the classifier's training domain.
- **Uniformity is a preference of the metric.** A balanced marginal maximizes its entropy, even when the target dataset has unequal class frequencies.

These are consequences of the score's definition. A high IS supports the statement that predictions are sharp and varied under this classifier; it does not establish complete coverage or fidelity to the target distribution.
