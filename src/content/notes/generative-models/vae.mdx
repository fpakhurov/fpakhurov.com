A variational autoencoder is a latent-variable model with a learned approximate posterior. It turns otherwise difficult posterior inference into a differentiable optimization problem.

## Evidence lower bound

For observation $x$ and latent variable $z$, introduce $q_\phi(z\mid x)$ as an approximation to $p_\theta(z\mid x)$. Then

$$
\log p_\theta(x) \ge
\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z\mid x)}[\log p_\theta(x\mid z)]
- D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_\phi(z\mid x)\,\|\,p(z)).
$$

The first term rewards reconstruction under the decoder. The second regularizes the approximate posterior toward the prior. Their balance shapes both fidelity and latent-space organization.

## Reparameterization

For a diagonal Gaussian posterior,

$$
z = \mu_\phi(x) + \sigma_\phi(x) \odot \epsilon,
\qquad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I).
$$

Randomness is isolated in $\epsilon$, leaving a differentiable path through $\mu_\phi$ and $\sigma_\phi$. This is the reparameterization trick.

<aside class="callout"><strong>Interpretation.</strong> A VAE does not simply compress an input. It learns a distribution over latent explanations while simultaneously learning how those explanations generate observations.</aside>
