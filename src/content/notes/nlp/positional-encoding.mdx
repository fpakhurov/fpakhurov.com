The [Transformer note](/notes/nlp/transformers) added a positional signal $P$ to the token embeddings before the first block and deferred what $P$ is. This note starts from why the signal is necessary, which takes one line of algebra, then builds the encoding used in the original paper and the alternatives that replaced it.

**Notation.** $X \in \mathbb R^{n \times d}$ the embedded sequence; $\Pi$ an $n \times n$ permutation matrix; $\mathrm{pos} \in \{0, \dots, n - 1\}$ a position; $P \in \mathbb R^{n \times d}$ the positional matrix with rows $P_{\mathrm{pos}}$.

## Baseline: no positions

Apply self-attention to a permuted input $\Pi X$. The projections are per row, so $Q, K, V$ become $\Pi Q, \Pi K, \Pi V$. Then

$$
\operatorname{softmax}\big((\Pi Q)(\Pi K)^\top / \sqrt{d_k}\big)\,\Pi V
= \operatorname{softmax}\big(\Pi\, Q K^\top \Pi^\top / \sqrt{d_k}\big)\,\Pi V
= \Pi\,\operatorname{softmax}\big(QK^\top/\sqrt{d_k}\big)\,V ,
$$

using $\Pi^\top \Pi = I$ and the fact that a row-wise softmax commutes with permuting rows and columns together. The output of the permuted input is the permuted output: self-attention is **permutation-equivariant**. The feed-forward layer, LayerNorm and residuals act per position and are equivariant too. The argument holds for unmasked attention, or for a mask permuted along with the tokens, as a padding mask is. A causal mask is attached to positions, not to tokens, so it breaks arbitrary permutations: in a decoder the first token attends only to itself, whatever word it is. An encoder has no causal mask, so an encoder without positional input computes, for "dog bites man" and "man bites dog", the same three vectors in a different order, and a classifier that pools them cannot tell the sentences apart. A recurrent network never had this problem, because it read tokens in sequence; the price of parallelism is that order must be put back by hand.

<aside class="callout"><strong>Key idea.</strong> Add to each token vector a second vector that depends only on the position, chosen so that different positions get different vectors and nearby positions get similar ones. Attention then sees position as part of the content it compares.</aside>

## Sinusoidal encoding

[Vaswani et al. (2017)](#references) define, for position $\mathrm{pos}$ and dimension index $i$,

$$
P_{\mathrm{pos},\,2i} = \sin\!\Big(\frac{\mathrm{pos}}{10000^{2i/d}}\Big), \qquad
P_{\mathrm{pos},\,2i+1} = \cos\!\Big(\frac{\mathrm{pos}}{10000^{2i/d}}\Big), \qquad i = 0, \dots, d/2 - 1 .
$$

Each pair of dimensions $(2i, 2i + 1)$ is a sine and cosine of the same angle $\omega_i \cdot \mathrm{pos}$ with $\omega_i = 10000^{-2i/d}$. The frequencies form a geometric progression: dimension pair 0 has wavelength $2\pi$ positions, the last pair, $i = d/2 - 1$, has wavelength $2\pi \cdot 10000^{1 - 2/d}$, which approaches $10000 \cdot 2\pi$ as $d$ grows: about 19,869 positions for $d = 16$ and 60,611 for $d = 512$. Low dimensions change quickly with position and resolve neighbours; high dimensions change slowly and encode coarse location.

### Worked example

$d = 4$, so two frequency pairs: $\omega_0 = 1$ and $\omega_1 = 10000^{-1/2} = 0.01$.

| pos | $\sin(\mathrm{pos})$ | $\cos(\mathrm{pos})$ | $\sin(0.01\,\mathrm{pos})$ | $\cos(0.01\,\mathrm{pos})$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 1 |
| 1 | 0.841 | 0.540 | 0.010 | 1.000 |
| 2 | 0.909 | −0.416 | 0.020 | 1.000 |
| 3 | 0.141 | −0.990 | 0.030 | 1.000 |

Rows are distinct, bounded in $[-1, 1]$, and the slow pair changes by $0.01$ per step, so positions 1 and 2 are closer in the slow dimensions than positions 1 and 30. The vector is added to the token embedding (scaled by $\sqrt d$ so that the two have comparable magnitude), and the same table is reused for every sequence; it is added once, before the first block.

### Properties

**Relative shift is a rotation.** For a fixed offset $k$, the pair at position $\mathrm{pos} + k$ is a linear function of the pair at $\mathrm{pos}$, independent of $\mathrm{pos}$:

$$
\begin{pmatrix} \sin\omega(\mathrm{pos} + k) \\ \cos\omega(\mathrm{pos} + k) \end{pmatrix}
= \begin{pmatrix} \cos\omega k & \sin\omega k \\ -\sin\omega k & \cos\omega k \end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix} \sin\omega\,\mathrm{pos} \\ \cos\omega\,\mathrm{pos} \end{pmatrix},
$$

which is the angle-addition identity. The authors hypothesized that this lets a linear projection in attention pick out relative positions; the dot product $P_{\mathrm{pos}} \cdot P_{\mathrm{pos}+k}$ indeed depends on $k$ only. Whether the trained model uses this is empirical.

**No parameters and no maximum length in principle.** The formula evaluates at any position. In practice models are trained on sequences up to some length and do worse beyond it, because the attention patterns learned on short sequences do not transfer to combinations of positional vectors never seen in training.

**Repetition within a dimension is not a collision.** Any single sine repeats, so positions $\mathrm{pos}$ and $\mathrm{pos} + 2\pi/\omega_i$ share dimension $2i$. The other dimensions have incommensurable periods, so two positions in any practical range do not share the whole vector.

## Learned absolute positions

Replace the formula by a table $P \in \mathbb R^{n_{\max} \times d}$ of free parameters, `nn.Embedding(max_len, d)`, trained with everything else. BERT does this with $n_{\max} = 512$, GPT-2 with 1,024, and the translation model of the [next note](/notes/nlp/transformer-machine-translation) with 32. It is simpler, performs about the same as sinusoids on tasks of the training length ([Vaswani et al., 2017](#references), Table 3, row E), and has a hard maximum: a position beyond $n_{\max}$ has no row. The table costs $n_{\max} \times d$ parameters, 0.4 M for BERT-base.

## Relative and rotary positions

Both schemes above encode *absolute* position and leave attention to infer relative distances. Later models encode the relative offset directly, because what matters for most linguistic relations is how far apart two tokens are, not where in the document they sit.

**Relative position biases** add a learned scalar $b_{j-i}$ to the score of query $i$ and key $j$ (T5, which shares one scalar among offsets grouped into buckets that widen with distance), or a fixed penalty proportional to $|j - i|$ (ALiBi, [Press, Smith and Lewis, 2022](#references)), with no positional vector at the input at all.

**Rotary position embeddings** (RoPE, [Su et al., 2024](#references)) move the sinusoids from the input to the attention scores. Each query and key of a head, of width $d_k$, is split into $d_k/2$ two-dimensional pairs, and pair $i$ at position $\mathrm{pos}$ is rotated by angle $\omega_i\,\mathrm{pos}$, with $\omega_i = 10000^{-2i/d_k}$. Because a dot product of two rotated vectors depends only on the *difference* of the rotation angles, the score $q_m \cdot k_n$ depends on the content of the two tokens and on the offset $n - m$, not on $m$ and $n$ separately. The shift property of the sinusoidal scheme becomes exact and built into every attention layer. Llama 3.2 1B, the model of the [GPT](/notes/nlp/gpt) and [instruction tuning](/notes/nlp/instruction-tuning) notes, uses RoPE.

### Why the rotated score depends only on the offset

Write $R(\alpha) = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\alpha & -\sin\alpha \\ \sin\alpha & \cos\alpha \end{pmatrix}$ for the rotation of the plane by angle $\alpha$. Take one coordinate pair of a query $q$ at position $m$ and of a key $k$ at position $n$, both after the projections $W_Q$ and $W_K$, and the frequency $\omega$ of that pair. RoPE replaces them by $R(m\omega)\,q$ and $R(n\omega)\,k$, and the score of the pair becomes

$$
\boxed{\big(R(m\omega)\,q\big)^{\top} R(n\omega)\,k = q^{\top} R\big((n - m)\,\omega\big)\,k .}
$$

Each step uses one fact about rotations:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\big(R(m\omega)\,q\big)^{\top} R(n\omega)\,k
&= q^{\top} R(m\omega)^{\top} R(n\omega)\,k && \text{transpose of a product} \\
&= q^{\top} R(-m\omega)\, R(n\omega)\,k && \text{a rotation's transpose is its inverse} \\
&= q^{\top} R\big((n - m)\,\omega\big)\,k && \text{rotation angles add.}
\end{aligned}
$$

The positions enter only through $n - m$, the content only through $q$ and $k$. A head of width $d_k$ does this for each of its $d_k/2$ pairs with its own $\omega_i$ and adds the results, so the full score is a sum of terms that each depend on the offset alone ([Su et al., 2024](#references), §3.2, eq. 16). The values are not rotated, and nothing is added to the token vectors.

**Worked example (toy frequency).** One pair with $\omega = 30^\circ$ per position, $q = (1, 0)$ and $k = (0.8, 0.6)$. Unrotated, $q \cdot k = 0.8$ and $k$ lies $36.87^\circ$ from $q$. (A real head uses $\omega_0 = 1$ radian, about $57^\circ$, for its fastest pair and far smaller angles for the others; $30^\circ$ keeps the arithmetic readable.)

| $m$ | $n$ | $n - m$ | $R(m\omega)\,q$ | $R(n\omega)\,k$ | Score |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 1 | 1 | $(1, 0)$ | $(0.393, 0.920)$ | 0.393 |
| 3 | 4 | 1 | $(0, 1)$ | $(-0.920, 0.393)$ | 0.393 |
| 7 | 8 | 1 | $(-0.866, -0.5)$ | $(0.120, -0.993)$ | 0.393 |
| 0 | 2 | 2 | $(1, 0)$ | $(-0.120, 0.993)$ | −0.120 |
| 3 | 5 | 2 | $(0, 1)$ | $(-0.993, -0.120)$ | −0.120 |

Both rotated vectors change from row to row, but the angle between them is $36.87^\circ + 30^\circ \cdot (n - m)$: $66.87^\circ$ for offset 1 and $96.87^\circ$ for offset 2. Both vectors have length 1, so the score is the cosine of that angle, $\cos 66.87^\circ = 0.393$ and $\cos 96.87^\circ = -0.120$. Moving both tokens by the same number of positions turns both vectors by the same angle and leaves the score unchanged.

### Why RoPE became the default

- **Relative by construction.** The score sees the offset, which is what most relations between tokens depend on, so a pattern learned at one place in a sequence applies at every other.
- **No parameters, no table.** There is nothing to learn and no row that runs out at $n_{\max}$.
- **The token vectors stay free of position.** Position enters only where queries meet keys. The residual stream and the values carry no positional vector, so position does not take up the dimensions that an added signal shares with the token vector (the second limitation below).
- **Compatible with the KV cache.** A key is rotated once, at its own position, when it is computed; appending a token leaves every cached key as it was.
- **Extendable to longer contexts.** Used far beyond its training length without change, RoPE degrades, since the attention scores at unseen angles can become very large ([Chen et al., 2023](#references)). Rescaling the angles fixes most of this with little training: *position interpolation* divides the positions by the extension factor and extends LLaMA models to 32,768 positions with at most 1,000 fine-tuning steps ([Chen et al., 2023](#references)), and Code Llama raises the base from 10,000 to 1,000,000 for a long-context fine-tuning stage ([Rozière et al., 2023](#references), §2.4).

All four schemes side by side:

| Scheme | Where applied | Parameters | Max length | Encodes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sinusoidal | Added to input | 0 | None in principle | Absolute, with shift structure |
| Learned table | Added to input | $n_{\max} d$ | $n_{\max}$ | Absolute |
| Relative bias / ALiBi | Attention scores | $O(n_{\max})$ or 0 | Extrapolates somewhat | Relative offset |
| RoPE | Queries and keys | 0 | Trained length, extendable | Relative offset in the dot product |

## What changed

| | Recurrent model | Transformer + positions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source of order | Sequential reading | An explicit positional signal |
| Cost | Implicit | $0$ to $n_{\max} d$ parameters |
| Parallel over positions | No | Yes |
| Length generalization | Any length, weak memory | Depends on the scheme |

## Limitations

- **Absolute schemes do not extrapolate.** A learned table has a hard limit; sinusoids have a soft one.
- **Added signals compete with content.** In the input-addition schemes the positional vector shares the $d$ dimensions with the token vector; the model must learn to separate them.
- **Long contexts stress every scheme.** Which positional method generalizes best to lengths beyond training is an active empirical question, and the answer interacts with the data and the attention implementation.
- **Positions are indices, not structure.** None of the schemes knows about sentences, paragraphs or syntax; those have to be learned from the tokens.

## What you should remember

- Unmasked self-attention is permutation-equivariant: $\operatorname{Attn}(\Pi X) = \Pi\,\operatorname{Attn}(X)$. Order must be added; a causal mask breaks the symmetry, since it is fixed by position.
- Sinusoidal: $\sin$ and $\cos$ of $\mathrm{pos} \cdot 10000^{-2i/d}$, wavelengths in geometric progression from $2\pi$ to $2\pi \cdot 10000^{1 - 2/d}$, just under $10000 \cdot 2\pi$ for large $d$; a shift by $k$ is a fixed rotation of each pair.
- Learned tables are simpler and bounded by $n_{\max}$.
- RoPE rotates queries and keys so that scores depend on relative offset: $\big(R(m\omega)\,q\big)^{\top} R(n\omega)\,k = q^{\top} R\big((n - m)\,\omega\big)\,k$. No parameters, no added vector, compatible with the KV cache, extendable by rescaling the angles; used by current decoder-only models.
- The scheme sets how the model handles lengths it was not trained on.
