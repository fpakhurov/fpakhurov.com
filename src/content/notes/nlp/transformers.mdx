Self-attention, as left by the [previous note](/notes/nlp/attention), produces for each token a weighted average of value vectors. That is a mixing operation across positions and nothing else: no non-linearity, no depth, no order, no way to generate. The Transformer ([Vaswani et al., 2017](#references)) is self-attention plus the minimum needed to fix each of these, and the result is a block that can be stacked, trained in parallel over the sequence, and arranged in three ways depending on the task.

**Notation.** $x \in \mathbb R^{n \times d}$ is the block input (one row per position), $d$ the model width, $d_{\text{ff}}$ the feed-forward width, $L$ the number of blocks, $h$ the number of heads. $\operatorname{Attn}$ is multi-head self-attention and $\operatorname{FFN}$ the position-wise feed-forward network.

## Baseline: a stack of attention layers

Stack self-attention layers directly and two things fail. Each layer's output is linear in its values, so $L$ layers compose into something that is still a (data-dependent) weighted average of the first layer's values; there is no per-token computation. And deep stacks of any kind suffer the gradient problems of the [RNN note](/notes/nlp/recurrent-networks#gradients-through-time): the signal at the top passes through $L$ transformations before reaching the bottom.

## The block

<aside class="callout"><strong>Key idea.</strong> Alternate two operations: attention moves information <em>between</em> positions, and a small two-layer network transforms each position <em>independently</em>. Wrap each in a residual connection so the block computes a correction to its input rather than a replacement, and normalize so that activations stay on a fixed scale however many blocks are stacked.</aside>

With pre-norm placement (the common modern choice) a block reads

$$
h' = h + \operatorname{Attn}(\operatorname{Norm}(h)), \qquad h'' = h' + \operatorname{FFN}(\operatorname{Norm}(h')).
$$

### Position-wise feed-forward network

$$
\operatorname{FFN}(x) = \sigma(x W_1 + b_1)\, W_2 + b_2, \qquad W_1 \in \mathbb R^{d \times d_{\text{ff}}},\; W_2 \in \mathbb R^{d_{\text{ff}} \times d},
$$

applied to every row of $x$ separately with the same weights. $\sigma$ is ReLU in the original and GELU in BERT and GPT-2; Llama replaces the two-matrix form by a gated one with three matrices (`gate`, `up`, `down` in the module listing) and the SiLU activation. The width is conventionally $d_{\text{ff}} = 4d$: 3,072 for BERT-base, 8,192 for a 2,048-wide Llama. This is the only non-linearity in the block and where most of its parameters live: $2 d d_{\text{ff}} = 8d^2$ against $4d^2$ for attention.

### Residual connections

$h + f(h)$ instead of $f(h)$. The derivative of the block output with respect to its input is $I + \partial f/\partial h$: the identity term gives every gradient a path that passes through no weight matrix, so stacking blocks does not multiply the gradient by $L$ Jacobians the way a recurrence did. This is why Transformers go deep: 12 blocks in BERT-base, 32 in a 7 B Llama, 96 in GPT-3, and the [translation note](/notes/nlp/transformer-machine-translation) gains 0.02 BLEU by going from 1 block to 4 on 29,000 sentences. The requirement is that input and output of every block have the same width $d$, which fixes $d$ throughout the stack.

### Normalization

**Layer normalization** ([Ba, Kiros and Hinton, 2016](#references)) standardizes each position's vector across its $d$ features:

$$
\operatorname{LN}(x_i) = \gamma \odot \frac{x_i - \mu_i}{\sqrt{\sigma_i^2 + \epsilon}} + \beta, \qquad \mu_i = \frac1d\sum_{j=1}^d x_{ij},\;\; \sigma_i^2 = \frac1d\sum_{j=1}^d (x_{ij} - \mu_i)^2 .
$$

Unlike batch normalization (see the [Generative Models note](/notes/generative-models/training-background#batch-normalization)), the statistics are per example and per position, so they do not depend on the batch and behave the same at training and inference time; this matters for variable-length sequences with padding. $\gamma, \beta \in \mathbb R^d$ are learned. RMSNorm, used by Llama, drops the mean subtraction and the $\beta$ and divides by the root mean square alone, which is cheaper and empirically as good ([Zhang and Sennrich, 2019](#references)).

### Pre-norm versus post-norm

The original paper normalizes *after* the residual sum, $h' = \operatorname{LN}(h + \operatorname{Attn}(h))$; this is the **post-norm** block, and it is what BERT uses and what the notebook implements. Most later models normalize the *input* of each sub-layer, the **pre-norm** block written above (GPT-2, Llama). The difference is where the residual path goes: in pre-norm it is an unbroken identity from the bottom of the stack to the top, in post-norm it passes through a LayerNorm at every block. [Xiong et al. (2020)](#references) showed that post-norm gradients at initialization grow with depth near the output, which is why post-norm models need learning-rate warm-up and careful initialization, whereas pre-norm trains stably without warm-up. Post-norm, when it trains, is sometimes slightly better on the final metric; the trade is stability for a small amount of accuracy, and at scale stability wins.

### Where the input comes from

Before the first block, token ids are embedded and position information is added: $x = E[\text{ids}] \cdot \sqrt d + P$, where $P$ encodes positions (the subject of the [next note](/notes/nlp/positional-encoding)). The $\sqrt d$ factor matches the scale of the embedding to that of the positional signal. Dropout is applied to the sum, to the attention weights, and after each sub-layer.

## Parameters of a block

Per block, ignoring biases and normalization vectors: $4d^2$ in attention (three projections plus the output map) and $8d^2$ in the FFN, $12d^2$ in total. The notebook's AG News model has $d = 32$ and, unusually, $d_{\text{ff}} = 32$:

| Component | Count |
| --- | --- |
| Token embeddings, $4{,}000 \times 32$ | 128,000 |
| Per block: attention $4 \times 32^2$ | 4,096 |
| Per block: FFN $2 \times (32 \times 32 + 32)$ | 2,112 |
| Per block: two LayerNorms, $2 \times 64$ | 128 |
| Four blocks | 25,344 |
| Classifier $32 \times 4 + 4$ | 132 |
| Sinusoidal position table $128 \times 32$ (fixed, but registered as a parameter) | 4,096 |
| **Total** | **157,572** |

The table matches the notebook's printed count exactly, and the breakdown shows the usual proportions: the embedding table dominates a small model, and the blocks are tiny. For BERT-base ($d = 768$, $L = 12$, cased vocabulary of 28,996) the same arithmetic gives $12 \times 12 \times 768^2 = 84.9$ M in the blocks, 22.3 M in token embeddings, 0.4 M in position embeddings and 0.6 M in the pooler, about 108 M in all, which is the figure the [NER note](/notes/nlp/named-entity-recognition) prints.

## Three arrangements

The same block is used in three ways, distinguished by the mask and by whether there is a second sequence.

**Encoder-only.** Blocks with unmasked self-attention: every position sees every other, in both directions. The output is one contextual vector per token. Used for representation and understanding tasks: classification by pooling the vectors (the notebook's model averages the non-padding positions; BERT uses the first token), token labelling, span extraction. The [BERT note](/notes/nlp/bert) is the main example.

**Decoder-only.** Blocks with causal self-attention: position $t$ sees positions $\le t$ only. The output at $t$ predicts token $t + 1$, so the model is a language model trained on whole sequences in one pass. The dominant arrangement for generation; the [GPT note](/notes/nlp/gpt).

**Encoder–decoder.** An encoder stack over the source, and a decoder stack whose blocks have a third sub-layer, **cross-attention**, in which the queries come from the decoder and the keys and values from the encoder output; this is the attention of the [pre-Transformer note](/notes/nlp/attention-before-transformers) with projections. The decoder's own self-attention is causal. Natural for translation and other input-to-output transformations; the [translation note](/notes/nlp/transformer-machine-translation) trains one.

The three names describe information flow. Tokenization, training objective, data, decoding and evaluation all remain to be chosen, and the [model-families note](/notes/nlp/model-families) compares them along those axes.

## A small encoder on AG News

To put a number next to the architecture: a 4-block, 4-head encoder with $d = 32$ over a 4,000-entry WordPiece vocabulary, mean-pooled over non-padding tokens into a linear classifier, trained from random initialization for 20 epochs with Adam at $10^{-3}$ and batch size 256, reaches **0.906** test accuracy on AG News. The TF-IDF baseline of the [bag-of-words note](/notes/nlp/bag-of-words-and-tf-idf) is 0.918 and the best convolutional model 0.911. A Transformer trained from scratch on 120,000 short documents is not better than the alternatives; its advantage appears when it is pretrained on far more text, which is the subject of the next section of the course. What the experiment does show is that the architecture trains without difficulty at this size and that its attention maps are inspectable.

## What changed

| | Self-attention alone | Transformer block |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Per-position computation | None (weighted average) | FFN with $8d^2$ parameters |
| Depth | Collapses to one mixing | $L$ residual blocks |
| Scale of activations | Uncontrolled | LayerNorm per position |
| Order | None | Positional signal added at the input |
| Generation | — | Causal mask in the decoder |
| Second sequence | — | Cross-attention |

## Limitations

- **Quadratic attention** is inherited; everything else in the block is linear in $n$.
- **Fixed width.** Residuals force one $d$ through the stack; capacity is added by depth or width everywhere.
- **Data appetite.** From scratch on small data, the architecture does not beat count baselines; it needs pretraining.
- **Position is an add-on.** Nothing in the block knows order; the quality of the positional scheme limits what the model can do with it.
- **Parameter count is not a design.** Two models with the same $L, d, h$ can differ in norm placement, activation, FFN form and positional scheme, and these change training behaviour more than the headline numbers suggest.

## What you should remember

- Block: $h' = h + \operatorname{Attn}(\operatorname{Norm}(h))$, $h'' = h' + \operatorname{FFN}(\operatorname{Norm}(h'))$. Attention mixes positions, FFN transforms each position, residuals keep an identity path, LayerNorm fixes the scale.
- $12d^2$ parameters per block ($4d^2$ attention, $8d^2$ FFN); the embedding table dominates small models.
- Pre-norm: identity residual path, stable deep training. Post-norm: original, needs warm-up.
- Encoder (bidirectional), decoder (causal), encoder–decoder (cross-attention): same block, different masks.
- From scratch on AG News: 0.906, below TF-IDF; pretraining is what makes the architecture pay off.
