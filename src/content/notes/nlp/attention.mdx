The [previous note](/notes/nlp/attention-before-transformers) let a decoder state read a weighted sum of encoder states. The two sides came from two recurrent networks, and the recurrence was still there: encoder states were computed one after another, and each carried its context only through the chain. This note removes the recurrence. A sequence attends to *itself*: every position scores every other position and aggregates their values, in one matrix product over the whole sequence, with nothing sequential left. This is the operation that the Transformer ([Vaswani et al., 2017](#references)) is built from.

**Notation.** A sequence of $n$ tokens with $d$-dimensional vectors is the matrix $X \in \mathbb R^{n \times d}$ (one row per token). Projection matrices $W_Q, W_K \in \mathbb R^{d \times d_k}$ and $W_V \in \mathbb R^{d \times d_v}$. Softmax over a matrix is taken row by row.

## Baseline: attention between two sequences

In the encoder–decoder, the *query* was a decoder state $s_t$, the things compared against it were the encoder states $h_i$, and the things summed were the same encoder states. Three roles, two sources, and the comparison vectors and the summed vectors were the same object.

<aside class="callout"><strong>Key idea.</strong> Give every token three vectors computed by three learned linear maps from its own representation: a <em>query</em> (what it is looking for), a <em>key</em> (what it offers for matching) and a <em>value</em> (what it contributes when matched). Then every token attends to every token of the same sequence. The same three maps are shared by all positions, so the whole computation is three matrix products, one score matrix and one softmax.</aside>

## From embeddings to Q, K and V

$$
Q = X W_Q \in \mathbb R^{n \times d_k}, \qquad K = X W_K \in \mathbb R^{n \times d_k}, \qquad V = X W_V \in \mathbb R^{n \times d_v}.
$$

Row $i$ of $Q$ is the query of token $i$, built from token $i$'s vector alone; likewise for keys and values. Separating the three roles matters: the vector a token uses to *search* need not be the vector it *presents* to searchers, and neither need be the information it *hands over*. In the previous note all three were the recurrent state; here they are three different projections of it.

## Scaled dot-product attention

$$
\operatorname{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \operatorname{softmax}\!\left(\frac{Q K^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V .
$$

Reading from the inside out:

1. $QK^\top \in \mathbb R^{n \times n}$. Entry $(i, j)$ is $q_i \cdot k_j$, the match between token $i$'s query and token $j$'s key. Row $i$ holds the scores of token $i$ against the whole sequence, including itself.
2. Dividing by $\sqrt{d_k}$ keeps the scores at a scale the softmax can work with (next subsection).
3. The row-wise softmax turns row $i$ into weights $\alpha_{i,1}, \dots, \alpha_{i,n}$ that are positive and sum to one, exactly as in the previous note.
4. Multiplying by $V$ makes row $i$ of the output $\sum_j \alpha_{i,j} v_j$, the weighted average of all value vectors from token $i$'s point of view.

| Matrix | Shape | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $X$ | $n \times d$ | token representations |
| $Q, K$ | $n \times d_k$ | queries, keys |
| $V$ | $n \times d_v$ | values |
| $QK^\top$ | $n \times n$ | token-to-token scores |
| $\operatorname{softmax}(QK^\top/\sqrt{d_k})$ | $n \times n$ | attention weights, rows sum to 1 |
| output | $n \times d_v$ | one context-aware vector per token |

The output has one row per input token: self-attention is a sequence-to-sequence map of the same length, which is what lets it be stacked.

### Worked example

Three tokens in two dimensions, $X$ with rows $(1, 0)$, $(0, 1)$, $(1, 1)$, and identity projections so that $Q = K = V = X$ and $d_k = 2$. The scaled scores $QK^\top/\sqrt 2$ are

$$
\frac{1}{\sqrt 2}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}
= \begin{pmatrix} .71 & 0 & .71 \\ 0 & .71 & .71 \\ .71 & .71 & 1.41 \end{pmatrix}.
$$

Row-wise softmax gives weights $(.40, .20, .40)$, $(.20, .40, .40)$, $(.25, .25, .50)$. The third token, whose query matches its own key best (score $1.41$), keeps half of its own value and takes a quarter from each neighbour; its output row is $.25\,(1,0) + .25\,(0,1) + .50\,(1,1) = (.75, .75)$. The first token's output is $(.80, .60)$: it started at $(1, 0)$ and moved towards the tokens it attended to. Every output row is a convex combination of the value rows.

### Why divide by $\sqrt{d_k}$

If the components of $q$ and $k$ are independent with mean 0 and variance 1, the dot product $q \cdot k = \sum_{m=1}^{d_k} q_m k_m$ is a sum of $d_k$ independent terms each with variance 1, so it has variance $d_k$ and standard deviation $\sqrt{d_k}$. With $d_k = 64$ a typical score is of order $8$, and a softmax over inputs that differ by several units is nearly one-hot. Two scores of $11.7$ and $-2.6$ give weights $0.9999994$ and $6 \times 10^{-7}$: the second token is effectively invisible, and the gradient of the softmax with respect to either score is of order $10^{-6}$, so training stalls. Dividing by $\sqrt{d_k}$ brings the scores back to unit variance regardless of $d_k$. The variance claim is an identity under the independence assumption; that this assumption roughly holds at initialization, and that the scaling helps in practice, is empirical ([Vaswani et al., 2017](#references), §3.2.1).

## Masks

Some scores must be excluded before the softmax, and the standard way is to set them to $-\infty$ so that $\exp(-\infty) = 0$ removes them from the normalization.

- **Padding mask.** Positions that are `[PAD]` should not receive attention: their keys are masked in every row. Shape $[B, 1, n]$, broadcast over queries.
- **Causal mask.** A language model predicting token $t + 1$ from tokens $\le t$ must not let position $t$ attend to positions $> t$; otherwise the answer leaks in. An upper-triangular mask of $-\infty$ above the diagonal, shape $[n, n]$, enforces it for all positions at once, which is what makes training on a whole sequence in one pass possible. The [GPT note](/notes/nlp/gpt) relies on this.

```python
scores = (q @ k.transpose(-2, -1)) / sqrt(d_k)        # [B, n, n]
scores = scores.masked_fill(~mask, float("-inf"))      # mask: True where allowed
weights = scores.softmax(dim=-1)                       # rows over allowed keys sum to 1
output = weights @ v                                   # [B, n, d_v]
```

A row with every key masked would be all $-\infty$ and produce NaN; padding masks must leave at least the token itself, or the row must be handled separately.

## Multi-head attention

One softmax per token gives one set of weights, so a token can express only one pattern of "who to look at" per layer. **Multi-head attention** runs $h$ independent attentions with smaller projections and concatenates the results:

$$
\operatorname{head}_m = \operatorname{Attention}(X W_Q^{(m)}, X W_K^{(m)}, X W_V^{(m)}), \qquad
\operatorname{MultiHead}(X) = [\operatorname{head}_1; \dots; \operatorname{head}_h]\, W_O ,
$$

with $W_Q^{(m)}, W_K^{(m)}, W_V^{(m)} \in \mathbb R^{d \times d/h}$ and $W_O \in \mathbb R^{d \times d}$. Each head has $d_k = d/h$, so the total cost in parameters and in floating-point operations is the same as one head of full width: $3 d^2$ for the projections plus $d^2$ for $W_O$, $4d^2$ per layer. For BERT-base, $d = 768$ and $h = 12$: $d_k = 64$ and $4 \times 768^2 = 2.36$ M attention parameters per layer. The small model trained in the notebook has $d = 32$, $h = 4$, $d_k = 8$.

Different heads can learn different relations (previous token, syntactic head, same entity), and plotting the $n \times n$ weights of each head in each layer of a trained model shows a mix of sharp local patterns in early layers and diffuse ones later. An individual head is not guaranteed to be interpretable, and many heads can be removed after training with little loss ([Voita et al., 2019](#references)).

## Complexity

The score matrix has $n^2$ entries per head, computed with $d_k$ multiplications each, and the product with $V$ costs the same:

| Operation | Time | Memory |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Projections $XW_Q, XW_K, XW_V$ | $O(n d^2)$ | $O(nd)$ |
| Scores $QK^\top$ and weights $\times V$ | $O(n^2 d)$ | $O(n^2)$ per head |
| Recurrent layer, for comparison | $O(n d^2)$, sequential in $n$ | $O(nd)$ |

For $n = 128$ and $d = 32$ the score matrices are $16{,}384$ entries per head per example and the projections dominate. For $n = 4{,}096$ and $d = 1{,}024$ the scores are $1.7 \times 10^7$ per head and $n^2 d \approx 1.7 \times 10^{10}$ operations per layer, larger than the $n d^2 \approx 4.3 \times 10^9$ of the projections; attention is the quadratic term. In exchange, nothing depends on a previous position: all $n$ rows are computed by one matrix multiplication, which is why a Transformer trains in parallel over the sequence and a recurrent network does not. Long-context systems therefore spend their engineering on this term: caching keys and values during generation, exact kernels that avoid materializing the $n \times n$ matrix ([Dao et al., 2022](#references)), and sparse, windowed or linearized variants.

## What changed

| | Encoder–decoder attention | Self-attention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Queries from | Decoder state | Every token of the sequence |
| Keys and values from | Encoder states (same vector) | Every token, by separate projections |
| Sequential dependency | Through both recurrences | None |
| Computation | One softmax per decoder step | One $n \times n$ softmax per layer |
| Cost | $O(n)$ per step | $O(n^2 d)$ per layer |
| Heads | One | $h$ in parallel |

## Limitations

- **No notion of order.** Permuting the input rows permutes the output rows identically; nothing in $QK^\top V$ knows that token 2 came before token 3. Order has to be injected separately, which the [positional representations note](/notes/nlp/positional-encoding) does.
- **Quadratic in $n$.** Doubling the context quadruples the score computation and memory.
- **Linear in the values.** The output is a weighted average of value vectors: there is no non-linearity per position yet, and a single attention layer cannot compute arbitrary functions of a token. The [Transformer note](/notes/nlp/transformers) adds the feed-forward layer for that reason.
- **Weights are not explanations.** Attention maps are a useful diagnostic and a poor proof of what the model relies on.

## What you should remember

- $Q = XW_Q$, $K = XW_K$, $V = XW_V$; $\operatorname{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \operatorname{softmax}(QK^\top/\sqrt{d_k})\,V$; output row $i$ is a convex combination of value rows weighted by how well query $i$ matches each key.
- $\sqrt{d_k}$ restores unit variance to the scores so the softmax does not saturate.
- Masks set forbidden scores to $-\infty$: padding keys always, future keys in a decoder.
- $h$ heads with $d_k = d/h$ cost the same as one wide head and give $h$ weight patterns per layer; $4d^2$ parameters per layer.
- Time $O(n^2 d)$, memory $O(n^2)$ per head, and no sequential dependency.

## Next

Self-attention mixes information across positions, with one weighted average per head. To build a deep model from it three more pieces are needed: a non-linear transformation applied to each position, a way to stack many layers without the gradient problems of the [RNN note](/notes/nlp/recurrent-networks#gradients-through-time), and the encoder, decoder and encoder–decoder arrangements that turn the block into a classifier, a language model or a translator.
