title: AI-first transformation architecture
summary: This one is less a system than a way of deciding what to build next. AI was often treated as a standalone feature, while the processes and data it depends on stayed implicit.
problem: Teams approached AI as an isolated feature. The processes, decision history and infrastructure it depends on remained implicit, so there was little for a model to learn from and little to evaluate it against.
context: AI adoption that lasts needs both a technical platform and an order of steps for turning work into something a model can learn from and people can govern.
role: I worked on the AI transformation strategy and on the architecture that connects business rules, datasets, MLOps and applied AI products.
approach: We used a maturity chain, from paper processes to agents, to find which foundations were missing and to decide what to invest in before any model-driven automation.
result: A shared framework for discussing where the organization is ready for AI and which prerequisite to strengthen next.
lessons: An agent cannot make up for missing process data or unclear business rules. The work starts with making decisions visible and recorded.
