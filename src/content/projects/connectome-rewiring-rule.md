description: Research into whether developmental synapse-formation rules learned from C. elegans connectomes transfer beyond development and provide a useful structural prior for learning.
highlights:
  - Eight developmental connectomes of C. elegans, from birth to adulthood (Witvliet et al., 2021).
  - Prediction of new synapses on held-out neuron pairs and on a future developmental stage.
  - Comparison against contact-geometry, published and null baselines, including a reproduction of the Richter–Schneidman model.
  - The learned rule, frozen before any task existed, tested as a structural prior in an independent learning experiment.
outcome: New synapses were predictable on unseen neuron pairs, above every baseline. As a frozen prior for learning, the rule gave no benefit over uniform or degree-matched priors in the tested setting, a negative transfer result. The work makes no claim about biological plasticity mechanisms.
